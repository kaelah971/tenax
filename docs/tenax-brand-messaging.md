# Tenax Brand Messaging

**Master brand:** Tenax · **Pronunciation:** `TEN-ax` · **Descriptor:** Event-aware protection for tokenized equity exposure. · **Tagline:** **Firm of purpose.**

This document is the working verbal system for Tenax. It consolidates the approved brand foundation and the seven-part scope into repeatable messages for the website, product interface, demo, pitch deck, and partner conversations.

---

## 1. Brand Core

### What Tenax is
Tenax is an AI protection agent for tokenized U.S. stock exposure. It interprets earnings and other high-risk events, proposes a bounded protective action, checks that proposal against a user-defined mandate in deterministic code, asks for approval, executes through a supported rail, and produces a decision receipt. It never takes custody of assets, and the model never holds execution authority.

### The idea to remember
**Reasoning without authority. Action without ambiguity.**

The user states the intent and the limits. The agent interprets and proposes. The mandate — not the model — decides what is permitted. Refusal is a legitimate answer. And every outcome, acted or refused, ends in a written record.

### Purpose
Make event-risk protection for tokenized equities trustworthy enough for ordinary holders — by never letting the reasoning layer hold unchecked authority over money.

### Vision
A world where any tokenized financial exposure can be protected by explicit, inspectable mandates — and where an AI's judgment is always paired with deterministic control and provable receipts.

### Mission
Turn a plain-language intent — "protect my NVIDIA through earnings" — into a bounded, approved, documented protection action, with refusal treated as a first-class outcome.

### Category
**Event-aware protection and bounded execution for tokenized equities.** Not "AI trading bot". Not "copilot". Both categories invite the wrong expectations.

### Emotional payoff
**Calm control:** "I don't have to watch the screen during earnings. My limits are watching instead — and I'll see the proof."

### Strategic enemy
**Blind agent authority** — systems that ask users to grant an AI unrestricted account control, hide their reasoning, and report only when a trade happened, never why it was allowed.

### Brand principles
- Reasoning ≠ authority. Judgment lives in the model; permission lives in code.
- Refusal is a feature. Restraint is a designed outcome, not an error.
- Never fake execution. Demo, dry-run and live states are always labeled.
- Preserve difference. Different tokenized representations are never collapsed into "the same asset."
- Receipts over claims. Show the chain: what was considered, what was allowed, what was rejected.
- Mandates stay visible. Authority is never buried in settings.

## 2. Audience and Jobs-to-be-Done

### Primary audience: holders of tokenized U.S. equities
Crypto-native investors and traders who hold rNVDA-style positions and want programmable, extended-hours access without manually watching every material event.

**Job:** "When an event could materially affect my tokenized stock exposure, help me understand it and prepare a bounded response without giving an AI unlimited control."

**What they need to believe:**
- The agent starts from what I economically own, not from a ticker string.
- The limits I set are enforced by code, not by good behavior.
- Doing nothing is a valid, visible result.
- Every outcome comes with a record I can read.

### Secondary audience: newer rToken holders
People who hold tokenized equities but do not live in trading terminals or trust black-box automation.

**Job:** "Show me protection in plain steps, tell me exactly what is allowed, and never surprise me."

**What they need to believe:**
- The screen explains itself without jargon.
- Approval is asked for in words I understand.
- Demo and dry-run are labeled honestly.

### Expansion audience: wallets, neobrokers, agent platforms
Teams that want a mandate-gated execution and protection layer they can embed.

**Job:** "Give us bounded authority our users and auditors can read — proposals, refusals, approvals, receipts."

**What they need to believe:**
- The mandate engine is deterministic and testable, independent of the model.
- The receipt is a complete decision trace.
- No custody; least-privilege execution; honest modes.

### Note on the visual language (in words)
Warm cream, one calm blue, soft light, and an isometric gate diagram: a proposal travels a path, passes a glowing gate, and ends in a receipt. The identity should look like an instrument, not a casino — protection you can read.

## 3. Positioning

### Positioning statement
> For holders of tokenized U.S. equities who cannot babysit every earnings event, Tenax is an event-aware protection agent that interprets material events, proposes bounded actions, and executes nothing without your mandate's permission. Unlike AI trading bots that hold account authority, Tenax separates reasoning from authorization — and proves both with a decision receipt.

### Simple positioning version
**Tenax protects tokenized stock exposure through earnings — with your limits enforced by code.**

### Value proposition
Set the intent and the limits once. Tenax reads the event, proposes the protection, checks it against your mandate, asks you before anything moves, and shows you exactly what happened — or why nothing should.

### Differentiation
- Exposure-aware: NVIDIA is understood as an economic object beneath rNVDA and other representations — differences preserved, never collapsed.
- Intent-driven: the user expresses an outcome, not a sequence of trades.
- Bounded AI: the model proposes; deterministic code authorizes. There is no path from reasoning to execution that skips the gate.
- Refusal-aware: NO ACTION is a first-class result with a recorded reason.
- Receipts: the decision chain — exposure, event, proposal, policy, approval, execution, rejected alternatives — is captured as proof.

### Reasons to believe
Where implemented, these are the proofs:

- The mandate is a visible, editable object: max percentage, max value, leverage off by default, approval required by default.
- The Mandate Gate runs as a pure function, independent of the LLM.
- Refusals name the failed rule and remove execution affordances.
- Decision Receipts snapshot the exact chain used at decision time.
- Execution modes are labeled: Bitget Demo paper execution or an explicit dry-run with "No funds moved."
- No custody; the system works through supported permissions only.

## 4. Messaging House

### Roof — core message
> **An agent you can trust with exposure, because it can never move beyond what you permitted — and it can prove it.**

### Pillar 1 — Understand the event, not just the ticker
**Message:** Tenax knows what you economically own before it proposes anything.

**Proof points:**
- rNVDA is interpreted as NVIDIA economic exposure.
- Known representations (rNVDA, NVDAx, Ondo) are mapped with their differences preserved.
- Event intelligence combines earnings, session and market context.
- The Exposure Graph and Event Room are visible screens, not slide promises.

**Customer outcome:** Protection that matches the real position — and understanding that survives representation changes.

### Pillar 2 — Reasoning that cannot act alone
**Message:** The AI proposes; your mandate decides. Those are different jobs, held by different systems.

**Proof points:**
- AI returns a structured proposal — or NO ACTION.
- The Mandate Gate checks percentage, notional, leverage, underlying and approval as plain code.
- The LLM has no execution path.
- A REFUSE state cannot execute; a PASS state still requires your approval.

**Customer outcome:** Delegation without a blank cheque.

### Pillar 3 — Refusal is a respectable outcome
**Message:** Sometimes the right result is doing nothing — and Tenax says so out loud.

**Proof points:**
- NO ACTION is displayed and explained, not hidden.
- Rejected alternatives are listed with reasons.
- The position simply stays as it was — and that is recorded.
- Refusals read as complete sentences, never as system accidents.

**Customer outcome:** Restraint you can trust and point to.

### Pillar 4 — Honest execution, provable outcomes
**Message:** What you see is what ran — and every run leaves a receipt.

**Proof points:**
- Demo vs dry-run is always visible; mode never changes silently.
- One approval authorizes exactly one action.
- The receipt records the full chain and the rejected alternatives.
- Data provenance and staleness are stated where they matter.

**Customer outcome:** Confidence that nothing happens off the record.

## 7. Verbal Identity

### Personality
- **Precise:** states checks, numbers and modes exactly.
- **Composed:** never breathless, never promotional under pressure.
- **Protective:** shows the boundaries without fearmongering.
- **Candid:** admits refusals, staleness, demo mode and limitations.
- **Calmly firm:** the confidence of a system that says "no" properly.

### Voice rules
1. **Name the rule, not the vibe.** "The proposal exceeds your $125 limit" — not "conditions made this action unsuitable."
2. **Show the chain.** "Checked: percentage, value, leverage, approval" — not "comprehensive validation passed."
3. **Treat refusal as a sentence, not an apology.** "Tenax refused this hedge: it exceeded the mandate." No hedging, no over-explaining.
4. **Never imply performance.** The product controls action quality under rules; it does not predict markets or produce returns.
5. **Label reality.** Demo, dry-run, live, stale, unavailable — the mode is stated every time, next to the thing it describes.

### Cadence
- Short declarative sentences for states and results.
- One fact per line inside gates and receipts.
- Plain words first, technical identifiers second.
- Numbers always concrete: `$102`, `25%`, `21 minutes`, `#0182`.

### Vocabulary to use
`exposure`, `intent`, `mandate`, `permission`, `propose`, `check`, `pass`, `refuse`, `no action`, `within your limits`, `decision receipt`, `demo`, `dry-run`, `provenance`, `stale`, `unavailable`.

### Vocabulary to avoid
`guaranteed`, `risk-free`, `safe returns`, `prevents losses`, `alpha`, `edge`, `autopilot` (as a safety claim), `fully autonomous`, `unrestricted`, `moonshot`, `can't lose`, `AI trader`, `trading bot`, `wealth machine`, `the future of finance` unless describing the thesis explicitly.

### Before and after
- **Before:** "Our autonomous AI engine intelligently protects your portfolio from earnings volatility."
- **After:** "Tenax read the earnings event, proposed a $102 hedge — within your $125 limit — and waited for your approval. Here is the receipt."

### State language
`Intent created` → `Analyzing event` → `Proposal ready` → `Mandate Gate: PASS / REFUSED` → `Awaiting approval` → `Approved` → `Executing (Demo)` / `Preview (Dry Run)` → `Completed` → `Receipt finalized`

Refusal paths: `Refused — over mandate %`, `Refused — over mandate value`, `Refused — leverage not permitted`, `Blocked — event data unavailable`, `No action — conditions unsuitable`.

## 8. Product Language and Surface Copy

### Navigation
`Capital` · `Events` · `Mandate` · `Activity` — plus the persistent environment line: `Bitget Demo` or `Dry Run` chip.

### Intent input
Placeholder: `Protect my NVIDIA through earnings — max 25%, max $125.`
Helper text: `Your limits stay editable until you approve an action.`

### Event Room
`Earnings approach` · `Affected exposure: $512 rNVDA` · `Agent status: assessment available` — CTA: `Protect this position`.

### Mandate summary
`You're allowing up to 25% of this position, up to $125, no leverage, with your approval before anything executes.`

### Mandate Gate
`Mandate Gate — CHECK 3 OF 5: NOTIONAL — $102 within your $125 limit → PASS.`
Refusal example: `REFUSED — the hedge exceeded your $125 limit by $40. No action was taken.`

### Approval
`One action, one approval: a $102 hedge on rNVDA (19.9% of position). Execution: Bitget Demo.`

### Execution
`Executing (Demo)…` · `Preview — No funds moved.` · `Execution Preview (Dry Run) — no funds move in this mode.`

### Decision Receipt
Header: `Decision Receipt #0182 — NVIDIA earnings — PASS — Approved — Demo execution.`
Rows: exposure · proposal · mandate result · approval · execution · rejected alternatives.

### Refusal copy
`Tenax refused this hedge: it exceeded the mandate. Your position is unchanged.` — always add the failed rule and the no-action statement.

### Empty and error states
- `No monitored exposure yet — connect Bitget Demo to see what you own.`
- `Event data unavailable. Tenax will not propose actions on stale data.`
- `Analysis unavailable — retry, or continue without a proposal.`

### Disclosures (required where relevant)
- `Bitget Demo — paper execution only.`
- `Execution Preview (Dry Run) — no funds moved.`
- `Not investment advice. Tenax executes only within your mandate.`

## 10. Hackathon and Demo Messaging

### The one-line story for judges
**"We did not build an AI trading bot. We built the authority layer around one — AI reasons, code authorizes, and every action or refusal ends in a receipt."**

### The 90-second demo, in beats
- **0–10s** — "I hold NVIDIA exposure through rNVDA on Bitget." Open the Capital dashboard.
- **10–20s** — Reveal the Exposure Graph: the agent understands economic exposure, not just a symbol.
- **20–30s** — Enter intent: "Protect this through earnings. Max 25%, max $125."
- **30–45s** — The AI assessment appears with reasons, signals and a bounded proposal.
- **45–55s** — The Mandate Gate checks in the open; a deliberately oversized alternative is REFUSED.
- **55–70s** — Approve one action; Demo execution or labeled dry-run runs.
- **70–85s** — The Decision Receipt proves the chain: what was permitted, rejected, executed.
- **85–90s** — Close: "Today this protects NVIDIA through earnings. The same engine can help capital hedge, borrow, earn or rebalance across tokenized markets."

### Say this, not that
| Say | Not |
|---|---|
| bounded authority | autonomous trading |
| the agent proposed; the mandate authorized | the AI decided |
| Tenax refused — here's the rule | the trade failed |
| Demo execution / dry-run preview | live trade (unless it truly is) |
| position unchanged — no action taken | nothing happened |

## 11. Claims Discipline and Compliance Guardrails

### Never claim
- Guaranteed protection, risk-free hedging, prevented losses, improved returns.
- That the model "decides" or "trades" — reasoning and authorization are separate and the language must keep them separate.
- Live execution when running in Demo or dry-run.
- Certainty about outcomes; the product controls process, permission and proof — not markets.

### Always disclose
- Execution mode (Demo / dry-run) wherever execution appears.
- Data provenance and staleness where data appears.
- No custody; supported permissions only.
- `Not investment advice.`

### Competition note
Reward language, promotional claims and submission copy must be checked against the Bitget AI Base Camp Hackathon S2 rules before publishing; this document governs tone and claims discipline, not prize mechanics.

## 12. Message Testing Checklist

### Five-second comprehension
Show the hero for five seconds, then ask: What is Tenax? What does it protect? Who approves?
Expected answer: *"It protects tokenized stock positions through events, and you approve before anything executes."*

### Mandate-language test
Show the mandate summary, then ask: What can Tenax do without asking you? What will it never do?
If a non-trader cannot answer both, simplify the summary language.

### Refusal comprehension test
Show a REFUSED state, then ask: Did anything move? Why not? What is the position now?
Everything should point to *"No action was taken; the rule was breached; the position is unchanged."*

### Category rejection test
Ask users to describe what Tenax is not. The desired answers include at least two of:
not a trading bot; not a chatbot; not a wallet; not a bridge; not a chain dashboard; not unrestricted automation.

### Naming test
Say "Tenax" once. Can the listener spell it and pronounce it? (TEN-ax.) If either fails, the deck should show the pronunciation guide on first use.

## Final Recommendation

Lead with **"Firm of purpose."** Pair it immediately with the one-liner: **"Tenax is an AI protection agent for tokenized stock exposure — it proposes within your limits, and only your mandate authorizes."**

Teach the chain everywhere it can fit: **Exposure → Intent → Intelligence → Mandate → Action or Refusal → Receipt.** That chain is the product, the demo, the interface, and the memory structure.
